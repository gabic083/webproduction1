About the Author

Feminist Horror. Queer Desire. Beautiful Decay.

Jenny Hval is a Norwegian writer, musician, and sound artist whose work moves between literature, music, and experimental performance. Originally from Norway, Hval studied creative writing and performance in Melbourne, Australia, before developing a career that refuses to fit neatly into one category. Her writing is often just as experimental as her music, exploring sexuality, identity, religion, and the strange relationship between the body and the world around us.

Her first novel, Paradise Rot, was originally published in Norwegian in 2009 and later translated into English in 2018. The novel follows Jo, a young woman studying abroad who moves into a strange house with another woman named Carral. As their relationship develops, the boundaries between bodies, plants, desire, and decay begin to blur. The book is sensual, surreal, and occasionally grotesque, turning an ordinary living situation into something that feels almost like a fever dream.

Hval followed Paradise Rot with Girls Against God, a genre-bending novel that mixes feminist theory, horror, witches, religious criticism, and a very strange version of 1990s Norway. Her writing often challenges traditional storytelling, moving between fiction, personal reflection, and political commentary. There is something particularly interesting about how she uses the grotesque and the uncomfortable to explore experiences that are usually pushed aside or treated as taboo. Her work feels less interested in giving readers answers than in making them question what they thought they understood.
