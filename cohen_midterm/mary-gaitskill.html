About the Author

Desire. Obsession. Uncomfortable Relationships.

Mary Gaitskill is an American novelist and short story writer born in Kentucky in 1954. Her fiction often explores sexuality, power, addiction, loneliness, and complicated relationships. Her characters are frequently flawed and difficult to understand, and her writing doesn't shy away from uncomfortable subjects.

Her first collection of short stories, Bad Behavior, was published in 1988 and established her as an important voice in contemporary American fiction. The collection includes "Secretary," a story about a young woman who enters a complicated relationship with her employer. It was later adapted into a 2002 film.

Gaitskill has also written novels including Two Girls, Fat and Thin and Veronica, which was a finalist for the National Book Award in 2005. Her writing is known for its psychological detail and willingness to examine subjects that are often ignored or considered taboo.

Explore Her Work

Bad Behavior is a good introduction to Gaitskill's writing. Its stories explore sex, relationships, and power in ways that are often uncomfortable but rarely predictable. Veronica offers a more extended look at memory, friendship, illness, and identity.
