About the Author

Carnival Freaks. Family Obsession. The Beauty of Being Strange.

Katherine Dunn (1945–2016) was an American novelist and journalist best known for her 1989 novel Geek Love. She lived and worked in Portland, Oregon, and also spent years writing about boxing for publications including Esquire, Sports Illustrated, and The New York Times Magazine.

Geek Love follows the Binewski family, a traveling carnival family whose parents deliberately engineer their children to become sideshow performers. Told largely through the perspective of Oly, an albino hunchback dwarf, the novel explores family loyalty, exploitation, obsession, and the line between what society considers normal and strange.

The book became a cult classic and was a finalist for both the National Book Award and the Bram Stoker Award. Dunn also wrote the novels Attic and Truck, as well as essays and journalism about boxing. Her work often focuses on outsiders, unusual subcultures, and people living beyond conventional ideas of respectability.
