About the Author

Alexandria. Displacement. Desire. The Complications of Belonging.

Noor Naga is an Egyptian-American writer and poet whose work explores identity, displacement, sexuality, and the complicated relationship between culture and belonging. Born in Philadelphia and raised in Dubai, she has lived across several countries and brings questions of geography, language, and cultural identity into much of her writing. Her work has appeared in literary publications and has received recognition for its experimental style and willingness to confront uncomfortable subjects.

Her first book, Washes, Prays, published in 2020, is a verse novel exploring religion, female sexuality, desire, and the contradictions of intimacy. Written through poetry and fragmented narration, the book follows a young woman navigating faith and relationships while struggling with the expectations placed on her. Naga's writing moves between humor, vulnerability, and discomfort, refusing to present desire or identity as something simple.

Her debut novel If an Egyptian Cannot Speak English, published in 2022, takes place in Cairo and follows an Egyptian-American woman who becomes involved with a local photographer. Their relationship develops into an unsettling examination of class, race, cultural fetishization, and the power dynamics between people from different backgrounds. The novel experiments with alternating perspectives, footnotes, and fragmented storytelling, constantly challenging the reader to question whose version of events can be trusted. It won the Center for Fiction First Novel Prize and the Graywolf Press Africa Prize.

Explore Her Work

Naga's writing is available through Graywolf Press and other independent publishers. Her novel If an Egyptian Cannot Speak English is particularly interesting for its experimental structure and complicated treatment of identity, desire, and cultural belonging.
