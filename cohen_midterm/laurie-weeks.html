About the Author

Downtown New York. Queer Obsession. Writing Without a Filter.

Laurie Weeks is an American writer, performer, and artist who became part of New York City's underground literary and art scene during the 1980s and 1990s. Her fiction and essays have appeared in publications including Vice, The Baffler, Nest, Index, and LA Weekly. She also worked as a screenwriter on the 1999 film Boys Don't Cry, adding another dimension to a career that moves between literature, performance, and independent culture.

Her debut novel Zipper Mouth, published in 2011, follows an unnamed young woman navigating addiction, unrequited love, and the chaotic world of downtown New York in the 1990s. Told through fragmented thoughts, letters, monologues, and memories, the novel has a deliberately unpredictable structure. Its narrator is obsessive, funny, self-destructive, and often difficult to follow. Rather than presenting a polished or particularly likable protagonist, Weeks allows the reader to experience the messiness of her inner world.

Zipper Mouth received the Lambda Literary Award for Lesbian Debut Fiction and has since become something of a cult favorite within queer literary circles. Weeks has described wanting to experiment with language and explore feelings that are difficult to express directly. Her writing draws from an unusual mix of influences, including punk culture, experimental literature, underground performance, and the strange details of everyday life. There is something refreshing about a writer who is so uninterested in making her stories neat or easily digestible.
